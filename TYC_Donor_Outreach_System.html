Individual donors

Community members, faith leaders, educators. They give from personal values, not spreadsheets. Lead with one story. Then bring the numbers.

The conversation flow

1

Connect personally 30 sec

"Hey [name], I volunteer with The Youth Complex here in Manchester. I know you care about kids in this community and I just wanted to share something real with you."

Tip: Use their name. Reference something specific you know about them. No pitch yet.

2

Tell one story 90 sec

"We have a kid who came in completely shut down. By month three he was leading our youth council. That is what we do. We give young people a space that actually sees them."

Tip: One specific, real story beats any statistic. Pick a youth you have actually seen change.

3

Drop the numbers 30 sec

"Right now we have 25 plus volunteers, 28 youth enrolled, 19 community partners. Zero paid staff. Every dollar goes straight to programming."

Tip: Numbers land after the story. Now they carry real weight.

ASK

Make the specific ask

"We are trying to raise $[X] for [specific thing]. Would you consider a gift of $[amount]? Even $50 covers a full month of supplies for one youth."

Rule: Tie every dollar to something concrete. Never say "anything helps."

5

Lock in a next step Before you leave

"No pressure at all. Can I send you something to look over? And would it be okay if I followed up in a week?"

Tip: A maybe later with no follow-up date is a no. Get a specific day before you leave.

All volunteer. 100% of donations go to programming

When they push back

"I do not know much about you."

That is exactly why I am here. Let me show you what we built in under a year.

"Money is tight right now."

Completely understood. Would you share us with one person who might be able to help?

"Let me think about it."

Of course. Can I follow up Thursday? I will send something over tonight.

The conversation flow

1

Lead with their reputation 30 sec

"Hi, I am [name] from The Youth Complex. I know [business name] is really rooted in this community and I wanted to talk about something that could be great for both of us."

Tip: Businesses respond to reputation, not sympathy. Never open with "we need help."

2

Show them their audience 45 sec

"We serve 28 youth and their families, work with 19 community partners, and have 25 active volunteers. These are all Manchester residents. Our events bring in parents, community members, and local leaders."

Tip: This is their customer base. Help them see it clearly.

ASK

Offer a specific tier

"We would love to list [business name] as a community sponsor. A sponsorship of $[amount] would fund [specific thing] and you would be featured across all our materials and events."

Rule: Give them a menu with named tiers. Businesses prefer structured options.

4

Mention the tax benefit briefly

"We are a registered 501(c)(3), EIN 33-1885285, so this is fully tax deductible. We will send a donation receipt right away."

Tip: Say it once, do not oversell it.

5

Always have a smaller ask ready

"If a donation does not work right now, would you be open to hosting a fundraiser night, or letting us put a flyer in your window?"

Tip: A flyer becomes a relationship. A relationship becomes a donation.

The conversation flow

1

Acknowledge what they already give

"You have already been one of our biggest supporters just by trusting us with your kid. That means everything. I wanted to talk with you about how you might help us reach more families like yours."

Tip: Never cold pitch a warm relationship. Recognize what they have already contributed first.

2

Show them the bigger picture

"Right now we have 28 youth enrolled. We know there are families in Manchester who need this and just do not know we exist yet. To grow, we need the community behind us."

Tip: Parents who have benefited become ambassadors. Plant that seed here.

ASK

The double ask: money AND referrals

"Would you consider a small monthly gift? Even $10 or $25 a month adds up fast when all of you are together. And just as valuable: is there another family you know who could use what we offer?"

Rule: Recurring $10/month = $120/year. One referral can become a long-term donor. Ask for both every time.

Word of mouth strategy

Ask them to share one TYC post per month
Give them 2 to 3 flyers to hand to other parents
Invite them to bring someone to the next event
Feature a parent quote on social with their permission

Make giving frictionless

Send a donation link they can set up in 2 minutes. Handle all the follow-up yourself so they never feel like they are selling anything.

Recurring donors are 3 to 5x more valuable over time than one-time givers. Always lead with the monthly option first.

Online and social donors

Instagram followers, Facebook community, email list. They give to moments, not campaigns. Create urgency with specific, time-bound needs.

The 3-post formula: rotate every week

Monday

The impact post

"This week [X] youth showed up to the Youth Complex. One came in stressed about school and left with a plan, a mentor, and a smile. That is what your support makes possible. Link in bio."

Wednesday

Behind the scenes

"Meet [volunteer name]. She gives [X] hours a week to TYC completely for free. She is one of 25 volunteers making this happen. Want to join us or support us? DM us or link in bio."

Friday

The specific ask

"We need $[X] by [date] for [specific thing]. We are at $[Y] so far. If 10 people give $[Z] each, we hit it. Can you be one of them? Link in bio, takes 2 minutes."

What actually converts online

Video. 30-second clips outperform photos by 3 to 5x
Progress bars. "We are 60% to our goal" drives action fast
Real faces. Named youth with permission, no stock images
Countdown. "3 days left" can double your click rate
Reply to every comment. Engagement signals trust to new visitors

Email follow-up sequence

Day 7. Impact update, show the money at work
Day 30. Story of one youth, brief and real
Day 60. Soft ask to upgrade to recurring or share with a friend
